# beat book guide notes — cat murphy, dec. 15

good lord, this was *a n n o y i n g* to make. only copilot could access all my files — with timestamps — and compile a chronological timeline of events, so my initial in-browser attempts, while well-intentioned, went just about nowhere. 

BUT. when i started using copilot to do this ... well, it was kind of embarrassing how well it parroted my voice and tone. hilarious, but also kind of mortifying — i mean, it's like reading something i wrote, except i mostly didn't. it was so uncanny that i actually had it split it up into "beatbook for dummies" and "my journey from the trenches" — which i later incorporated into a website — because it was such a weird mix of narrative and guide, but both had their advantages. so i kept both.

oh, and it also created one document named "cat_murphy_iteration_analysis.md." it referred to me in the third person and was generally odd, but it ran through every script i had run and showed what did/didn't work. like a technical guide. it wasn't *unhelpful* ... just kind of weird.

also, hilariously, it really liked to make shit up, even here. like, bro. you have ALL the documentation you could need — WHY are you making up code and prompts i know i didn't use????? bizarre. 

ok, then i decided to turn it into a fun webpage.

but here was the main issue. i tried at one point to get claude (in-browser) to clean up the html, but it just wouldn't stop making stuff up. i specifically asked it to ONLY modify the hierarchy and ux, but it would completely rewrite the text, and i ended up just having to restore an older version and have copilot fix it — which required significantly more iteration, because copilot is clunkier about almost everything. for some reason it really struggled with making my journey from the trenches page not have literally 17 subheaders.  took me an hour to get it to restructure it without deleting the text — it was super weird.

annnnnyway. i landed on this approach for my guide: incorporate both a practical guide and a walkthrough of my actual process. i was going to add an appendix, too, but i'll get to that.

here's my reasoning, and note that it's somewhat selfish. the practical guide is what a person who is trying to build *one* beat book would want — short, relatively simple, easy enough to read, not super technical. but at the same time, i personally wanted a blog post-style guide. i wanted something that did more than just tell you what to do. i wanted it to take you through MY process, good and bad, and not just the process that worked. 

i included all the things in my practical guide that i thought would be helpful for someone just ... you know, trying to do their own version. it's relatively generic in the sense that it sets expectations and gives pointers on how to go about things, but it doesn't really hone in on *my* results.

yeah, well, for my purposes — and i guess for anyone who wants to dive deeper into **how** i got there — i wanted a step-by-step guide on exactly what not to do. i wanted to give an idea of what i tried that didn't work. what failure actually looked like. how i got from one thing to the next. how each version improved upon the previous one.

and i low-key just wanted this for me. i want to be able to go back and look at what i did — particularly regarding the prompts — without going through all my files.
