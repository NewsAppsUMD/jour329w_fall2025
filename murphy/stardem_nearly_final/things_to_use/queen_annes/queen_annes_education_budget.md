# Queen Anne's County Education Budget Data (FY2026)

*Extracted from budget analysis documents*

## Education Spending Overview

**Total Education Budget:** $83,373,301 (39.9% of total county budget)
- **Largest single budget category**
- **Change from FY2025:** +$8,288,082 (+11.0%)
- **Above Maintenance of Effort:** +$7,615,140

## Year-Over-Year Trends

### Two-Year Growth Pattern

| Metric | FY2024 Actual | FY2025 Budget | FY2026 Budget | 2-Year Growth |
|--------|---------------|---------------|---------------|---------------|
| **Education Spending** | $68.9M | $75.1M | $83.4M | **21.0%** |
| Education % of Budget | 35.7% | 38.9% | 39.9% | +4.2 points |

**Key Finding:** Education's share of the budget has grown from 35.7% (FY2024) to 39.9% (FY2026).

## Funding Above Requirements

### Discretionary Local Investment
- **Maintenance of Effort (MOE) requirement:** State-mandated minimum
- **Queen Anne's actual funding:** $7,615,140 **above MOE requirement**
- This represents significant discretionary local commitment beyond state mandates

## Budget Controls

### Special Provisions
- Budget adopted **by category** (rather than lump sum)
- Requires **commissioner approval** for transfers between categories
- Board of Education required to meet **quarterly with County Finance Office**
- More oversight than typical county department

## Capital Investment - Education

### FY2026 Capital Projects: $8,483,460

**Major Components:**
- **HVAC replacements:** $3,600,000
- **Fire alarm systems:** (included in total)
- **Field lighting:** (included in total)

### Multi-Year Capital Outlook
The six-year capital plan (FY2026-2031) includes education infrastructure as part of **$466.3 million total capital investment**, indicating:
- Deferred maintenance catch-up
- Growth-driven expansion needs
- Aging school facility upgrades

## Revenue Sources Supporting Education

### Tax Rates (Unchanged from FY2025)
- **Property Tax:** $0.83 per $100 assessed value
- **Income Tax:** 3.20% (county piggyback)

### Property Tax Revenue
- **FY2026:** $89,466,500 (42.8% of total revenue)
- Growth driven by assessment increases, not rate hikes

### Income Tax Revenue
- **FY2026:** $89,500,000 (42.8% of total revenue)
- Nearly equal to property tax
- Reflects economic growth

**Combined:** These two sources (85.6% of revenue) largely fund the education budget increase.

## Fiscal Sustainability

### Structural Balance Achievement
The budget achieves balance **without using fund balance reserves**:
- FY2025 used $1.13 million fund balance
- FY2026 uses $0 fund balance
- Indicates improved structural balance despite spending increase

### Debt Service
- **FY2026:** $13,356,594 (6.4% of budget)
- Declined from 6.6% (FY2024) as % of total spending
- Indicates fiscal capacity for increased operational spending (like education)

## Comparative Context

**Education as % of Budget:**
- Queen Anne's: **39.9%** (highest among five counties)
- Talbot: 38.1%
- Caroline: 29.2%
- Dorchester: ~26-31%
- Kent: 26.1%

## Economic Indicators

### Assessment Growth
- Property tax revenue: +$6.8M (FY2025 to FY2026)
- Driven by rising property assessments
- Provides revenue growth without rate increases

### Development Activity
- Recordation tax: $7 million (indicates continued real estate activity)
- Transfer tax: $2.6 million (healthy property turnover)
- Building permits: $880,375 (stable construction activity)

**Translation:** Strong economy supports ability to increase education funding.

## Analysis & Context

### "Aggressive Investment"
The budget document describes this as **"aggressive investment in education"** while maintaining:
- Unchanged tax rates
- Structural balance without gimmicks
- No fund balance draw
- Fiscal discipline

### Revenue Assumptions Signal Optimism
- Interest income projection: $5.5 million (up 120% from FY2025)
- Reflects higher cash balances and interest rate environment
- Property/income tax growth assumes continued economic stability

### Long-term Sustainability Factors
1. **Debt service manageable** at 6.4% of budget
2. **OPEB funded** per 10-year plan ($4.46M)
3. **Capital pay-as-you-go** funding ($596,576) supplements bonding
4. **Conservative revenue estimates** in volatile categories

## Key Strengths

1. **Highest education investment** (as % of budget) among five counties
2. **Funding above state requirements** by $7.6M
3. **Structural balance** achieved without reserves
4. **Tax rates unchanged** despite 11% education spending increase
5. **Strong capital program** for school facilities ($8.5M)

## Policy Implications

### Budget Priorities Clear
Growing education share of budget (35.7% → 39.9% over 2 years) indicates:
- County prioritizes education over other services
- Willingness to shift resources to meet Blueprint mandates
- Economic growth enables investment without tax increases

### Oversight Structure
Categorical budgeting and quarterly meetings suggest:
- County commissioners maintain close oversight
- Flexibility limited compared to lump-sum approach
- Accountability mechanisms in place

## Bottom Line

Queen Anne's County demonstrates **fiscally sustainable education investment**:
- Largest education budget (as %) among peer counties
- 21% growth over two years
- $7.6M above state requirements
- Achieved without tax increases or using reserves
- Strong capital program for facilities
- Economic growth enables investment

**Contrast with peers:** Unlike Caroline (using reserves), Kent (structural deficit), or Dorchester (tax increases), Queen Anne's achieves education investment through economic growth and fiscal discipline.
