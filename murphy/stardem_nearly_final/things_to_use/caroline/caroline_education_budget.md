The 6-year capital plan shows **$71 million for schools** but only **$532K allocated in FY2026**. This suggests major school construction projects are deferred to future years.

## Analysis & Context

### Education Cost Acceleration
- K-12 spending jumped **$2.7 million (13%)** in one year
- **Fastest growth rate** of any major budget category
- Outpaces overall county budget growth of 9.5%

### State Mandate Pressure
- Maryland's "Blueprint for Education" mandates requiring increased local funding
- State grants increased by $456,076 (+7.6%) but commissioners note this is "entirely offset by increased mandates"
- Budget message explicitly mentions "reduced funding for programs from the state" alongside increased mandates

### Capital Investment Timing
- Minimal education capital spending ($532K) in FY2026
- Recreation/parks receives $6M in capital funding (heavily grant-funded)
- Suggests county is **deferring school capital needs** while Blueprint mandates grow
- Creates compounding problem: deferred needs + increasing operating costs

### Fiscal Implications
- Education consumes 29.2% of operating budget
- Commissioners warn current spending growth pace is "unsustainable"
- County using 15 years of accumulated surplus to bridge gaps
- FY2027 or FY2028 likely when difficult decisions must be made

### Comparative Context
- Caroline education spending (29.2%) is between Kent (26.1%) and Dorchester (31-35%)
- Per the analysis: "The county is deferring school capital needs while warning about unsustainable operating costs. This creates a compounding problem."

## Budget Pressures

1. **Operating costs growing faster than revenue**
   - 13% education increase vs. 9.5% overall revenue growth
   
2. **Capital needs deferred**
   - $71M in total education capital projects identified
   - Only $532K funded in current year
   
3. **State mandates increasing**
   - Blueprint for Education requirements
   - State funding not keeping pace with mandates
   
4. **Limited fiscal flexibility**
   - Tax rates unchanged (Property: $0.98 per $100; Income: 3.2%)
   - Relying on reserve funds to maintain services
