# star dem nearly final — cat murphy, dec. 1

i've decided to go back to education as a topic for the nearly final/final version, just because i think it's more manageable — local government is so sprawling, and i feel like i have a better grasp of education. also, i want to apply the critiques/suggestions directly as opposed to adjusting them to fit the local government topic.

as for fact-checking, because my draft is 40+ pages long and each chapter is structurally the same, i fact-checked in full the first chapter of my education enhanced v2 book. i was relatively impressed by the accuracy of my draft, but there were also some fatal flaws in that department. it was really good with data that i gave it separately — mcap scores, demographic information, etc. 

it was good, but not quite as good, with data that was available only in the article content itself. i’m again talking here about statistics and numbers. there was one instance that i found where it incorrectly cited the percentage increase in student homelessness in talbot county. the number in the text was very clearly 39%, but the llm cited 45%. 

the biggest issue was not with data, though — in fact, it’s pretty clear that feeding it key data separately almost guarantees that it will get those facts correct. at the same time, it really struggled with quotes and citations. i’m not sure that any of the “direct quotes” or headlines it pulled were entirely accurate — they were all at least partially wrong. many of the headlines it cited were *close*, but the llm would randomly carve out or paraphrase some of it. by far its favorite thing to do, though, was to directly quote — although sometimes misquote — paraphrases. this was consistent across most of its “quotes,” which makes me wonder if it’s tripping up on the structure of a typical quote: “blah blah blah,” last name said. i say this because the majority of the paraphrases it “quoted” were structured like, “full title full name emphasized that blah blah blah.” also, it tended to lean toward quoting the llm-generated summaries rather than the articles themselves. then again, it also just straight up invented some quotes, which isn’t great. 

one of the other critiques i got was that the beatbook was overall kind of repetitive — which, fair. in another draft, i would probably eliminate the current county-by-county structure and instead have it do a “three key issues on the education beat” for the eastern shore overall, and then add a county-by-county section that gives the recent coverage themes for each county. another one of the suggestions i got was about the citations — as in, the beatbook keeps referencing stories using theoretical footnotes but then never actually defines the footnotes. even when it tried to reference the story’s headline relative to the footnote, it would misstate the headline or quote parts of it but paraphrase the rest. the misquoting and lack of citations are my biggest concerns as far as a final draft. i feel like it has never been great at citing things, but the completely making up quotes is concerning. 

i wonder if pulling the quotes out into a separate document — organized by topic — might help? i’d hope that assigning full names to quotes would give it a more concrete idea of who said what. but also, i don’t really know if there’s a perfect solution for the paraphrasing thing. i think prompting would help, but i don’t imagine that would solve it completely. also, i don’t necessarily WANT it to completely exclude paraphrased quotes, but i don’t want it to present them as direct quotes. 

another comment i got was about enrollment data at the school level, which, again, fair. to be honest, i had tried to do this — i think it would allow for better analysis of scores vs. demographics. i would want to find a way to include census tract data in there as well. but the msde website uses microsoft power bi to present each school’s data and it’s TERRIBLE for scraping. however, while working on the “nearly final” version, i managed to get claude (through a mix of in-browser use and via copilot) to build me a better scraper using javascript. it’s slow, to be sure, but i have successfully scraped the enrollment data from more than half of the 50 k-12 schools in our five counties. i still need to scrape the rest, and i also have a separate scraper for absenteeism rates and demographic data not related to race or gender (students with disabilities, free and reduced meals students, etc., which is for some reason on a separate tab on msde’s website), but i have yet to run that one. that being said, once i do, i have a feeling the best course of action is similar to what i did for the blueprint data and budget data — make a separate book that synthesizes the data, and feed that to the llm to take into consideration alongside the story data. 

one of the other critiques i found interesting was the one on higher education in these counties. i don’t disagree, but im also not sure if there’s enough content to really make a meaningful section on it. i’d have to look at the stories to see what is available and if there are any overarching themes or ongoing stories (specifically with chesapeake college) that might be worth noting. 

the biggest thing on the beatbook production side of things — and i noticed this in particular while fact-checking my draft — is that the story data is impossible to parse manually. we need a better format, whether that’s in a datasette or something else. it would be impossible in the current form for a reporter to go back and reference any of the (supposedly) cited documents. on that note, i wonder if prompting the llm to cite by row id would be helpful — like, “story #25” or whatever, and then having a datasette with those stories. idk. i’m pretty intrigued by the separating out quotes idea. overall, i’m not displeased with this draft. but given the critiques i got and my experience fact-checking it, i also see a lot of things it could use. i also definitely agree that it would probably be better on a website or something. however, my theory of the case is that the best option would be a yes and: make the beatbook as we have been, then have the llm synthesize the info for a webpage that highlights the overarching themes but includes page number references and story ids so that the reporter can then go back and look at exactly what they’re looking for either in the beatbook or in a story.
