## 1. County Overview  

Queen Anne’s County is a largely suburban‑rural jurisdiction on Maryland’s Eastern Shore with a 2022 ACS five‑year estimated population of **50,316**. The median age is **44.6 years**, reflecting an older‑than‑state average. The gender balance is near‑even (25,045 male; 25,271 female). Racial‑ethnic composition is predominantly White (**42,958**), with Black residents numbering **2,952** and a Hispanic/Latino population of **2,303**.

Economically, the county is affluent relative to national figures. Median household income stands at **$108,332**, and the labor force includes **27,224** workers, of whom **987** are unemployed, yielding an unemployment rate of roughly **3.6%**. The **poverty rate** is **6.9%** (about **3,419** residents), well below the national average.

Housing stock is characterized by high ownership and high values. **Homeownership** is **81.4%**, with a **median home value** of **$421,900**. Rental units command a **median rent** of **$1,600**, but **47.9%** of renters are cost‑burdened (spending 30 % or more of income on rent).  

Broadband penetration is relatively strong at **86.4%**, indicating most households have high‑speed internet access. Health insurance coverage is also high; the **uninsured rate** is **2.9%**, well under the national average.

Educational attainment is notable for a high share of advanced degrees. Residents holding a **bachelor’s degree** number **8,244**, **master’s** **3,623**, **professional** **774**, and **doctorate** **549**, suggesting a well‑educated electorate that may influence policy priorities such as education funding and skilled‑workforce development.

## 3. Key Policy Areas & Challenges  

### Infrastructure – Wastewater & Water Systems  
- **Issue:** The Kent Narrows/Stevensville/Grasonville wastewater treatment plant has reached capacity, prompting a 12‑month moratorium on new residential development in affected sub‑districts.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** FY2026 capital budget allocates **$12.35 M** to the Sanitary District (including Phase III & IV upgrades). The overall general‑fund budget is **$209 M**, with **$15.6 M** for Public Works.  
- **Recent Developments:** Moratorium enacted Aug 12 2025; a task force created to review water/sewer allocation. Bond issuance of **$39 M** (including $26 M new money) will fund sewer expansions such as Southern Kent Island Phase 4 (211 properties, $6.81 M contract).  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Restricting development protects existing infrastructure but may constrain tax‑base growth and housing supply. Funding large‑scale upgrades competes with other priorities like education and public safety.

### Housing Affordability & Cost‑Burden  
- **Issue:** Nearly half of renters (**47.9%**) are cost‑burdened, while home values remain high (**$421,900 median**).  
- **Budget/Data Context:** Community Services budget grew **19.4%** to **$5.67 M**, with allocations to the Area Agency on Aging, new Public Housing Authority funding (**$829,703**), and Housing & Community Services (**$815,715**).  
- **Recent Developments:** The Board approved additional funding for the Public Housing Authority and expanded community services to address affordable‑housing gaps.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Resources directed to affordable housing reduce the fiscal space for other services; high property values limit the tax base for new affordable‑housing initiatives without increasing development pressure.

### Public Safety & Emergency Services  
- **Issue:** Maintaining adequate emergency response capacity amid population growth and infrastructure projects.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** Public Safety receives **$24.66 M** (11.8% of the budget), a **4.6%** increase YoY. Emergency Services budget alone rose **$733,522** to **$16.82 M**.  
- **Recent Developments:** FY2026 budget includes a modest increase for the Detention Center (+$344,484) and the Sheriff’s Office (+$1.29 M). No major policy shifts noted in minutes.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Funding increases must be balanced against rising community‑services costs and capital project financing; any further expansion could pressure the overall budget.

### Education Funding  
- **Issue:** Sustaining high‑quality K‑12 education while meeting state Maintenance of Effort (MOE) requirements.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** Board of Education receives **$83.37 M** (39.9% of FY2026 budget), an **11%** rise (+$8.29 M). The county contributes **$7.62 M** above MOE, indicating discretionary local investment.  
- **Recent Developments:** FY2026 budget emphasizes increased education spending, reflecting the county’s highly educated populace (over 22% hold a bachelor’s degree or higher).  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Education’s large share limits flexibility for other services; growth in school enrollment may later require additional facility funding, competing with infrastructure and housing needs.

### Environmental Regulation & Development Controls  
- **Issue:** Managing growth while complying with Total Maximum Daily Load (TMDL) regulations for water quality.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** Capital projects for sanitary district and wetlands mitigation consume a sizable portion of the **$48.6 M** capital budget.  
- **Recent Developments:** The 12‑month moratorium and the pursuit of a plant rerating (additional 300,000 gpd) illustrate regulatory constraints on development.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Environmental compliance restricts new housing and commercial projects, potentially limiting revenue growth; however, failure to address water quality could incur state penalties and degrade community health.
