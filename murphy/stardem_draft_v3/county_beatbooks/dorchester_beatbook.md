## 1. County Overview  

Dorchester County had an estimated **32,557** residents in the 2022 American Community Survey. The median age is **43.8 years**, reflecting a relatively mature population. The gender split is modestly tilted toward females (17,296) versus males (15,261). Racial‑ethnic composition is predominantly White (20,381), with Black residents accounting for 8,342 and Hispanic/Latino residents numbering 1,992.  

Economically, the county’s **median household income** is **$57,490** and the **labor force** comprises 15,877 workers, of whom 870 are unemployed, giving an unemployment rate of roughly **5.5 %**. Poverty remains a concern: **15.4 %** of residents (≈ 4,951 people) live below the federal poverty line.  

Housing is characterized by a relatively high **homeownership rate (68.4 %)**. The **median home value** stands at **$226,000**, while the **median monthly rent** is **$968**. However, **47.4 %** of renters are cost‑burdened (paying 30 % or more of income), indicating affordability pressures among renters.  

Broadband penetration is strong at **85.7 %**, suggesting most households have internet access. Health insurance coverage is also high, with only **3.4 %** of the population uninsured.  

Educational attainment shows a modestly educated adult population: **2,425** hold a bachelor’s degree, **1,381** a master’s, **379** a professional degree, and **283** a doctorate. These figures translate to roughly **7.5 %** of adults with a bachelor’s degree or higher, underscoring a workforce that is less college‑educated than the state average.

## 3. Key Policy Areas & Challenges  

### Infrastructure – Roads & Bridges  
- **Issue:** Critical repairs needed on the **Worlds End Turn** and other state‑maintained arteries; seasonal flooding threatens **Eldorado Road/Puckum Road**.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** FY 2026 **Other Taxes** (recordation, 911 fees) rose **62 %** to $7.08 M, partly reflecting higher real‑estate activity and the need for infrastructure funding. The county increased **property tax** revenue by $2.5 M (6.5 % growth).  
- **Recent Developments:** Council secured a **$1 M** state‑funded construction contract for Worlds End Turn, scheduled for summer 2026 with a 3‑4 week full closure. MDOT officials were pressed for firm timelines.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Balancing agricultural access (tractor tonnage limits) against the urgency of road safety; allocating limited capital funds while the **capital budget** fell 63 % to $1.69 M.

### Housing Affordability & Homelessness  
- **Issue:** High renter cost‑burden (47.4 %) and a growing homeless population.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** FY 2026 **Licenses & Permits** revenue grew 61 % to $472 K, indicating heightened development activity. The county allocated **over $1.9 M** in grants for shelters, youth programs, and health‑service initiatives under the “Moving Dorchester Forward” plan.  
- **Recent Developments:** Grants awarded to local service providers (e.g., Delmarva Community Services, ALL4LOVE Inc.) to expand temporary shelter capacity and wrap‑around services. No recorded public opposition in council minutes, suggesting low visible contention or limited awareness.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Funding homelessness programs reduces the pool for other priorities (e.g., education cuts); reliance on grant dollars raises questions about sustainability.

### Education Funding  
- **Issue:** Significant reduction in the education budget.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** FY 2026 education spending declined **$3.75 M (‑13 %)** to $24.92 M, the largest cut among major categories. This occurs despite a **23.9 %** share of total expenditures.  
- **Recent Developments:** No explicit council action noted in the minutes; the budget reflects a re‑allocation toward enterprise funds and a new “Miscellaneous” line item of $11.62 M.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Cuts may affect school programs, while the county seeks to meet fiscal targets after a **$13.7 M** overall budget increase.  

### Economic Development & Grants  
- **Issue:** Balancing growth incentives with fiscal restraint.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** **Grants Budget** surged **166 %** to $10.60 M, signaling a strategic push for external funding. **Other Taxes** increased 62 % and **Licenses & Permits** up 61 % reflect a more active development pipeline.  
- **Recent Developments:** The county pursued multiple grant awards for homelessness and health services; the Pinks Pond development proposal sparked the first organized community opposition, indicating heightened public scrutiny of new projects.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Attracting developers may conflict with environmental concerns raised by residents; the council must weigh short‑term tax revenue gains against long‑term land‑use sustainability.

### Environmental Regulation & Land Use  
- **Issue:** Wetland protection and infrastructure resilience amid climate‑related flooding.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** No dedicated environmental line item appears in the FY 2026 budget, but increased **recordation tax** revenue suggests higher property transactions, often tied to land‑development proposals.  
- **Recent Developments:** Community speakers at the November 4 meeting highlighted potential impacts of the Pinks Pond project on wetlands and municipal services.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Development proponents argue for economic benefits, while environmental advocates emphasize ecosystem preservation and flood mitigation.
