## 1. County Overview  

Kent County is a small, predominantly White jurisdiction on Maryland’s Eastern Shore with a 2022 ACS five‑year population estimate of **19,289**. The median age is **48.5 years**, reflecting an older resident base. Gender distribution is fairly balanced (9,215 male; 10,074 female). Racial composition is 78 % White (15,082), 14 % Black (2,729), and about 5 % Hispanic/Latino (900).  

Economically, the county’s **median household income** stands at **$71,635**, which exceeds the national median but must be interpreted against a **poverty rate of 9.5 %** (≈1,693 residents). The labor force comprises 9,679 workers, of whom **315 are unemployed**, yielding an unemployment rate of roughly 3.3 %.  

Housing is characterized by a **homeownership rate of 69.8 %**. The **median home value** is **$291,900**, while the **median monthly rent** is **$1,072**. Despite relatively high homeownership, **49.3 % of renters are cost‑burdened** (spending ≥30 % of income on rent).  

Broadband penetration is **79.4 %**, indicating that roughly one in five households lacks reliable high‑speed internet. Health insurance coverage is near universal, with an **uninsured rate of 1.6 %**.  

Educational attainment shows **2,939 residents with a bachelor’s degree**, **1,474 with a master’s**, **404 with professional degrees**, and **397 with doctorates**. These figures suggest a modestly educated adult population relative to the county’s size.

## 3. Key Policy Areas & Challenges  

### Infrastructure – Schools & Capital Projects  

- **Issue:** Funding shortfalls and state‑formula inequities jeopardize the construction of a new middle school.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** FY 2026 education allocation is **$22.3 M (26.1 % of total budget)**; **$15.8 M** of the FY 2026 “Transfers to Other Funds” category is earmarked for capital projects, including school construction. The county’s reliance on a **$17.3 M fund‑balance transfer** to balance the overall budget underscores limited operating revenue flexibility.  
- **Recent Developments:** October 7, 2025 – Board approved a letter of intent for the middle‑school project with a contingency clause tied to “fair and equitable State funding.” October 28 – FY 2027 Capital Improvement Program (CIP) submitted with the same contingency language.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Balancing state‑funding formulas against local tax capacity; delaying construction could increase costs, while proceeding without full funding would raise the local tax burden.

### Infrastructure – Water & Wastewater  

- **Issue:** Aging water‑tower infrastructure and the need for a regional wastewater treatment plant.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** Water‑tower maintenance project budgeted at **$358,000**; the Millington regional wastewater plant is slated for construction in 2026 with critical‑path funding. The water‑and‑wastewater enterprise fund receives **$4.3 M** via transfers, part of the **$20.7 M** total “Transfers to Other Funds.”  
- **Recent Developments:** September 23 – KCI Technologies provided design status; October 21 – Community Development Block Grant application submitted; ongoing property acquisition.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Capital outlays compete with other infrastructure priorities (roads, public safety) and rely heavily on state/federal grants.

### Public Safety & Emergency Services  

- **Issue:** Maintaining robust law‑enforcement, fire, and emergency‑medical capabilities amid budget constraints.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** Public safety receives **$16.5 M (19.4 % of total)**. Breakdown: Law enforcement **$4.99 M**, Corrections **$4.28 M**, EMS **$3.40 M**, Volunteer fire companies **$1.37 M**.  
- **Recent Developments:** November 4, 2025 – Approval of a new **$25,611** headquarters for emergency services; continued funding for volunteer fire companies.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Allocation to public safety limits flexibility for other services (e.g., social services **$0.9 M**) and capital projects.

### Housing Affordability & Cost‑Burden  

- **Issue:** High renter cost‑burden (49.3 %) despite strong homeownership rates.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** Housing‑related expenditures are embedded in **General Government** (planning, zoning, housing **$1.38 M**) and **Public Works** (environmental operations **$1.44 M**). No dedicated affordable‑housing line item is identified.  
- **Recent Developments:** No specific housing initiatives appear in the FY 2026 budget or meeting minutes.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Funding for new housing initiatives would need to be sourced from either reallocation of existing categories or additional revenue (property taxes already constitute **56 %** of operating revenue).

### Healthcare & Social Services  

- **Issue:** Providing public health services with limited dedicated funding.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** “Conservation of Health” receives **$1.37 M (1.6 % of total)**, covering the health department and mosquito control. Social services are allocated **$0.76 M (0.9 % of total)**.  
- **Recent Developments:** No major health‑policy actions noted in the meeting minutes.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Small health budget relative to population size may limit capacity to address public‑health gaps, especially given the aging median age (48.5 y).
