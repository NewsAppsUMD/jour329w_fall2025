## 1. County Overview  

Caroline County’s 2022 American Community Survey estimates show a total population of **33,320** residents, with a near‑even gender split (16,414 male; 16,906 female) and a median age of **39.5 years**. Racial‑ethnic composition is predominantly White (≈ 75 % – 25,025 people), with Black residents accounting for about **14 %** (4,565) and Hispanic/Latino residents representing roughly **8 %** (2,736).  

Economically, the county’s **median household income** stands at **$65,326**, while the **median home value** is **$258,800**. The labor force comprises **16,808** workers; **775** are recorded as unemployed, yielding an unemployment rate of roughly **4.6 %** (unemployment figure not expressed as a rate in the source). **Poverty** affects **12.9 %** of the population, or **4,228** individuals.  

Housing is characterized by a high **homeownership rate of 72.1 %**. Renters face a **median monthly rent of $1,074**, and **48.3 %** of renters are cost‑burdened (spending ≥ 30 % of income on housing). Broadband penetration is **85.6 %**, indicating that a sizable minority still lacks high‑speed internet. Health‑insurance coverage is strong, with an **uninsured rate of 4.1 %**.  

Educational attainment shows **2,362** residents with a bachelor’s degree, **1,335** with a master’s, **259** with a professional degree, and **88** with a doctorate. These figures suggest a modestly educated workforce relative to state averages, with the bulk of the adult population holding a high‑school diploma or some college without a degree.

## 3. Key Policy Areas & Challenges  

### Detention‑Center Infrastructure  
- **Issue:** The county’s jail is aging and cannot accommodate mandated medical and behavioral‑health services, prompting a shift from an $8 M renovation concept to a full **$32‑$40 M replacement**.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** Jail operating budget is **$6.02 M** (FY 2026), up **2.2 %** from FY 2025. Capital‑budget allocation for the Jail this year is **$25,911**, a fraction of the projected replacement cost, indicating future financing gaps.  
- **Recent Developments:** In November 2025 the Commissioners created a Detention Center Design Committee and began evaluating state cost‑share options (potentially 50 %).  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Large capital outlay competes with other priorities (e.g., roads, parks). Uncertainty about state matching funds creates fiscal risk for the county’s debt capacity.  

### Wastewater & Environmental Regulation  
- **Issue:** State‑mandated updates to wastewater treatment and solar‑energy regulations require county‑wide compliance upgrades.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** Public‑Works (Roads) receives **$2.51 M** (8.6 % increase); broader Public‑Works (including infrastructure) is funded **$7.61 M** in the capital budget.  
- **Recent Developments:** The fall 2025 meetings approved revisions to the county’s solar‑permit code and authorized a wastewater‑system feasibility study.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Investment in environmental compliance draws on limited capital resources, potentially delaying other infrastructure projects.  

### Housing Affordability & Cost‑Burden  
- **Issue:** Nearly **half of renters (48.3 %)** are cost‑burdened, while homeownership remains high but median home values have risen modestly.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** No dedicated housing‑affordability line item appears in the FY 2026 operating budget; the **Recreation & Parks** capital allocation ($6.06 M) is the largest single capital spend, suggesting limited direct funding for affordable‑housing initiatives.  
- **Recent Developments:** The County has not introduced new affordable‑housing programs in the recent budget cycle, but discussions in the meetings referenced the need to monitor CDBG‑funded homelessness services.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Allocating funds to affordable housing would require re‑prioritizing from other capital projects (e.g., parks, roads) or seeking external grants.  

### Public Safety & Emergency Services  
- **Issue:** Maintaining adequate staffing and equipment for the Sheriff’s Office, Jail, EMS, and fire companies amid modest budget growth.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** Sheriff budget **$6.01 M** (+8 %); Jail **$6.02 M** (+2.2 %); EMS **$5.73 M** (+4.7 %). Combined, public‑safety operating expenditures total **$15.05 M**, representing **19 %** of the operating budget.  
- **Recent Developments:** FY 2026 includes a **$1.50 M** capital line for Emergency Services (e.g., communications upgrades). The Board approved a Mobile Integrated Health program targeting high 911‑utilizer patients.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Funding for expanded EMS/health‑integration competes with the looming detention‑center replacement and with debt‑service obligations (**$3.68 M**, 4.6 % of the budget).  

### Education Funding  
- **Issue:** The Board of Education’s budget grew **13 %** to **$23.14 M**, now consuming **29.2 %** of the operating budget, reflecting state mandates and local enrollment trends.  
- **Budget/Data Context:** Education is the single largest expense category. No capital‑budget allocation for new school construction appears this cycle, though the Board of Education received **$532,222** in FY 2026 capital funds (down from $952,181 in prior years).  
- **Recent Developments:** The FY 2026 budget includes higher per‑pupil operating costs; the Board is monitoring opioid‑settlement funds for supplemental school‑based programs.  
- **Tensions/Trade‑offs:** Maintaining education spending at current levels pressures other departments, particularly when state grant growth (≈ 7.6 %) is modest relative to education cost growth.
