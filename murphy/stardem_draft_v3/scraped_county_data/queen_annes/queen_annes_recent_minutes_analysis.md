# **Queen Anne's County Commissioners Briefing: Key Issues, Dynamics & Public Response**  
**Period Covered: August 9 – October 28, 2025**

## **Executive Summary**
Queen Anne's County faces significant infrastructure and financial pressures as it navigates a $39 million bond issuance, major sewer projects, and unprecedented development restrictions. The Board of County Commissioners has imposed a 12-month moratorium on residential development in key growth areas due to wastewater treatment capacity constraints, while simultaneously advancing critical infrastructure projects including wetlands mitigation, Southern Kent Island sewer expansion, and the assumption of Sudlersville's water and sewer systems.

Public sentiment reveals growing concern over development impacts, with organized opposition emerging around immigration enforcement (287g program) while infrastructure needs generate consistent taxpayer engagement. The county achieved historic AAA bond ratings from all three major agencies, providing financial flexibility amid these challenges.

## **Major Issues & Developments**

### **1. Development Moratorium and Infrastructure Crisis**
**The Crisis:**  
The Kent Narrows/Stevensville/Grasonville Wastewater Treatment Plant has reached capacity, prompting an emergency 12-month moratorium on residential and mixed-use developments exceeding 1,750 gallons per day (approximately 7 single-family homes) in affected subdistricts.

**Financial Impact:**  
The county has applied for a plant rerating that could add 300,000 gallons per day capacity. However, federal Total Maximum Daily Load (TMDL) regulations make further expansion unlikely.

**Timeline**
- **Moratorium enacted:** August 12, 2025  
- **Duration:** 12 months  
- **Rerating decision:** Pending  

**Public Response:**  
Limited public comment suggests either lack of awareness or resignation. Joe Stevens and Joe Cusimano commented on August 12; no organized opposition emerged.

**Board Response:**  
Unanimous approval. A task force was established to review water/sewer allocation policies and school capacity formulas.

### **2. $39 Million Bond Issuance and Historic AAA Rating**
**The Achievement:**  
Queen Anne's County became the only Eastern Shore county to achieve AAA ratings from all three major bond rating agencies—a historic first.

**Financial Details**
- **Total bond authorization:** $39,000,000  
- **New money portion:** $26,000,000  
- **Interest rate reduction:** 3.17% → 2.51%  
- **Purpose:** Various capital and infrastructure projects  

**Board Dynamics:**  
Commissioners demonstrated clear unity and pride, repeatedly highlighting the achievement.

### **3. Sudlersville Water/Sewer System Acquisition**
**The Transaction:**  
County assumption of the Town of Sudlersville's water and sewer facilities, including **$5.5 million in debt**.

**Financial Structure**
- **Debt assumption:** $5,500,000  
- **USDA loan transfer included**  
- **New subdistricts:**  
  - Sudlersville/Barclay Wastewater  
  - Sudlersville Water  

**Timeline**
- **Public hearing:** September 23, 2025  
- **Ordinance adopted:** September 23, 2025  
- **Lease executed:** September 23, 2025  

**Public Impact:**  
No public comments were made.

### **4. Southern Kent Island Sewer Expansion – Phase 4**
**Project Scope:**  
Installation of STEP systems for 211 properties in four neighborhoods.

**Financial Details**
- **Winning bid:** $6,812,720 (Schummer, Inc.)  
- **Next lowest bid:** $7,597,670  
- **Properties served:** 211  

**Board Decision:**  
Conditional award pending MDE approval.

### **5. Immigration Enforcement Controversy**
**Issue:** Debate over the 287g program allowing local law enforcement to perform certain immigration enforcement functions.

**Public Sentiment**
- **Opposition:**  
  - John Ramsey (Sept. 23)  
  - Daniel McCue (Sept. 9)  
- **Support:** None recorded  

**Board Response:**  
No formal action taken to date.

## **Analysis & Implications**
The county faces a perfect storm of infrastructure limitations, development pressure, and financial constraints. Wastewater capacity issues have forced growth restrictions during a period of high housing demand. While the AAA rating offers financial breathing room, it cannot overcome federal environmental limits.

The board’s unity may signal genuine consensus or an avoidance of public disagreement. Meanwhile, the lack of comment on major financial decisions raises concerns about civic disengagement.

Opposition to immigration enforcement introduces a new political tension, while the community’s limited response to the moratorium suggests residents may not yet understand its long-term impacts.
