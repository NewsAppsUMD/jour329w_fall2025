# Queen Anne's County FY2026 Budget Analysis

## Executive Summary

Queen Anne's County approved a $209 million budget for fiscal year 2026 (beginning July 1, 2025), representing an 8.2% increase from the FY2025 revised budget of $193.1 million. The budget maintains all existing tax rates while significantly increasing funding for education and public safety.

## Tax Rates (Unchanged from FY2025)

| Tax Type | Rate | Notes |
|----------|------|-------|
| **Real Property Tax** | $0.83 per $100 assessed value | Lower rates for Centreville ($0.70) and Millington ($0.745) |
| **Personal Property Tax** | $2.075 per $100 | For utilities and electricity generation |
| **Income Tax** | 3.20% | County piggyback tax on state income tax |
| **Recordation Tax** | $4.95 per $500 | $0.15 goes to Critical Workforce Program |
| **Transfer Tax** | 0.5% | Reduced to 0.25% for first-time Maryland homebuyers |
| **Hotel Tax** | 5% | Applied to transient accommodations |

## Policy Provisions and Fiscal Management

### Revenue Dedications
1. **Solar Generation Tax Revenue**: Specifically earmarked for farmland preservation programs
2. **Recordation Tax Split**: $0.15 of $4.95 rate dedicated to Critical Workforce Program
3. **Agricultural Transfer Tax**: $1.5 million directed to preservation efforts

### Fiscal Controls
- Volunteer Fire Companies permitted to carry forward capital allocations with separate accounting
- Three-year expenditure requirement on capital project funds
- Board of Education required to meet quarterly with County Finance Office
- Reversions budgeted at negative $2.26 million to balance spending

### Fund Balance Management
The budget achieves balance without using fund balance reserves, compared to $1.13 million fund balance use in FY2025. This indicates improved structural balance despite the spending increase.

## Comparative Analysis

### Historical Context
| Metric | FY2024 Actual | FY2025 Budget | FY2026 Budget | 2-Year Growth |

| Total General Fund | $193.1M | $193.1M | $209.1M | 8.3% |
| Property Tax Revenue | $79.4M | $82.7M | $89.5M | 12.7% |
| Education Spending | $68.9M | $75.1M | $83.4M | 21.0% |
| Debt Service | $12.7M | $12.8M | $13.4M | 5.5% |

### Spending Priorities Shift
Education's share of the budget has grown from 35.7% (FY2024) to 39.9% (FY2026), while debt service has declined from 6.6% to 6.4% of total spending, indicating fiscal capacity for increased operational spending.

## Economic Indicators

### Revenue Assumptions Signal Optimism
- Interest income projection of $5.5 million (up 120% from FY2025) reflects higher cash balances and interest rate environment
- Property tax growth assumes continued assessment increases
- Income tax projection of $89.5 million suggests economic stability

### Development Activity Markers
- Recordation tax revenue projected at $7 million indicates continued real estate activity
- Transfer tax at $2.6 million suggests healthy property turnover
- Building permits revenue stable at $880,375

## Structural Budget Features

### Balanced Without Gimmicks
The FY2026 budget achieves structural balance through:
- Natural revenue growth rather than rate increases
- No fund balance appropriation (compared to prior year use)
- Conservative revenue estimates in volatile categories
- Contingency reserve of only $151,000 (0.07% of budget)

### Long-term Sustainability Factors
- Debt service remains manageable at 6.4% of budget
- OPEB (Other Post-Employment Benefits) funded per 10-year plan at $4.46 million
- Capital projects pay-as-you-go funding at $596,576 supplements bonding

This budget represents aggressive investment in education and services while maintaining fiscal discipline through unchanged tax rates and structural balance. The significant capital program addresses infrastructure needs while the operational budget responds to demographic and service demands.
