## CAROLINE COUNTY

### Demographics & Population

**Total Population:** 33,406 (2023)[^1]  
**Median Age:** 39.8 years[^1]  
**Racial/Ethnic Composition:**
- White (Non-Hispanic): 72.8%[^1]
- Black/African American (Non-Hispanic): 13.2%[^1]
- Hispanic/Latino: 8.77% (2,930 people)[^2]
- Two or More Races: 3.06%[^1]

**Immigration & Language:**
- Foreign-born population: 6.8% (2,271 people)[^3]
- This represents an increase from 6.29% in 2022[^2]
- Persons speaking language other than English at home: 6.8%[^3]

**Economic Indicators:**
- Median household income: $66,368 (2023)[^1]
- Poverty rate: 12.4% overall; 19.5% of children (2024)[^2]
- School budget ($100.4M) exceeds county operating budget ($79.2M)[^4]

### Educational System

**District Overview:**
- Total enrollment: 5,400 students[^4]
- Number of schools: 9[^4]
- Superintendent: Derek L. Simmons[^4]

**Funding Structure:**
- Total per-pupil spending: $19,796[^4]
- Local per-pupil: $3,760[^4]
- State per-pupil: $14,630[^4]
- State aid dependency: 78.8%[^4]
- Local share: only 20.2%[^4]

**Academic Performance:**
- Caroline County graduation rate: 86% (2023-2024)[^5]
- This is slightly above the state average of 85.8%[^5]
- 100% of Caroline schools rated 3 stars or above on 2024-2025 Maryland Report Card[^6]

**High-Need Schools:**
- Greensboro Elementary: 50% Hispanic enrollment, 87.7% FARMS[^4]
- Federalsburg Elementary: 38.55% African American, significant poverty concentration[^4]

**Key Challenges:**
- Heavy Blueprint dependency limits local flexibility[^4]
- School operating budget exceeds entire county general fund[^4]
- Multiple Community Eligibility Provision (CEP) schools indicate concentrated poverty[^4]
- Rising special education costs and utility expenses strain limited local resources[^4]

## Key Regional Findings

### Immigration & Demographic Change
All five counties experienced growth in foreign-born populations from 2022 to 2023, with Talbot County leading at 8.51% foreign-born residents[^17]. However, all five counties remain well below Maryland's statewide foreign-born rate of 16.3%[^20]. Hispanic populations range from 5.26% in Queen Anne's County to 9.1% in Talbot County, with Caroline County at 8.77%[^2][^12][^17].

### Educational Equity Paradox
Wealthier counties (Queen Anne's, Talbot) achieve higher graduation rates but exhibit concerning discipline disparities. Queen Anne's County's 3.8x discipline disparity rate and Talbot's 3.0x rate indicate that Black students face disproportionate exclusionary discipline despite living in counties with superior resources[^16].

### Blueprint Implementation Challenges
Caroline County exemplifies the "Blueprint dependency" crisis: its school operating budget ($100.4M) exceeds the entire county general fund ($79.2M), creating unsustainable reliance on state aid at 78.8%[^4]. Any reduction in state funding would force catastrophic cuts.

### Small District Disadvantage
Kent County, despite high per-pupil spending ($21,188) and low state dependency (33.5%), faces structural disadvantages from small scale. With only 1,800 students across 5 schools and just 66 teachers, the district cannot achieve economies of scale or offer programming diversity available in larger systems[^4][^8].

### Population Growth Patterns
All five counties showed population growth from 2020-2024, with Caroline, Dorchester, Kent, Queen Anne's, and Talbot all experiencing consistent annual increases[^21]. Queen Anne's County led regional growth at 8.1% from 2020-2024[^21].
