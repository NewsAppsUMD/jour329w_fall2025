# Talbot County Education Budget Data (FY2026)

*Extracted from budget analysis documents*

## Education Spending Overview

**Total Education Budget:** $61,447,701 (38.1% of total county budget)
- **Largest single budget category**
- Includes Board of Education, teacher pensions, and debt service

### Breakdown:
- **Board of Education:** Primary operating budget
- **Teacher Pensions:** $440,098
- **Education Debt Service:** $5,300,000
- **Chesapeake College:** $2,000,000

## Budget Context

### Education as Top Priority
- **38.1% of total general fund** goes to education
- **Second highest** among five counties (after Queen Anne's at 39.9%)
- Reflects county policy priorities

## Capital Investment - Education

### FY2026 Capital Projects: $260,500
- Relatively modest capital allocation compared to other categories
- County focusing capital on other infrastructure needs

### Multi-Year Context
Total Capital Projects Program for all county facilities: $15.9 million

**Major capital initiatives (non-education):**
- Water/Sewer Extension: $5 million
- 911 Technology & CAD Replacement: $1.3 million
- County-wide roads: $1 million
- Tunis Mills Bridge: $2.8 million

**Implication:** Education capital needs may be addressed through separate bonding or deferred to future years.

## Revenue Sources

### Tax Structure (Minimal Changes)
- **Real Property Tax (County):** $0.7926 per $100
- **Real Property Tax (Education Supplement):** $0.0106 per $100 (applied countywide)
- **Income Tax:** 2.40% (county piggyback rate)

### Revenue Breakdown
| Revenue Type | FY2026 Amount | % of Total |
|--------------|---------------|------------|
| **Property Taxes** | $68,661,616 | 42.6% |
| **Income Tax** | $39,500,000 | 24.5% |
| **Prior Year Reserves** | $21,656,693 | 13.4% |

**Combined property + income tax:** 67.1% of total revenue largely funds education.

## Fiscal Sustainability Concerns

### Reserve Dependence
Budget achieves balance using **$21.7 million in prior year reserves** (13.4% of budget).

**Analyst's observation:** "Heavy reliance on prior year fund balance ($21.7M) raises questions about long-term sustainability"

### Structural Balance Questions
- Contingency reserve: $1.75 million (only 1.1% of budget)
- No significant tax rate increases
- Relying on one-time money (reserves) to balance

**Implication:** When reserves are depleted, education funding may face pressure.

## Comparative Context

**Education as % of Budget:**
- Talbot: **38.1%**
- Queen Anne's: 39.9% (highest)
- Caroline: 29.2%
- Dorchester: ~26-31%
- Kent: 26.1%

**Analyst's key observation:** "Education-Centric Budget: Nearly 40% of the budget goes to education, reflecting county priorities"

## Debt Service

**Total debt service:** $2.6 million (1.6% of operating budget)
- Maintains manageable debt levels
- Low debt service provides capacity for future bonding if needed

## Enterprise Funds - Impact on General Fund

**Easton Airport:** $5.9 million operating budget
- Runway improvements: $10.6 million capital
- Federal grants covering 94.5% of capital costs
- Self-sustaining; minimal impact on general fund available for education

**Sanitary Districts:** $7.7 million budget
- Major capital: $38.7 million watershed improvements
- Self-sustaining through user fees
- Doesn't compete with education for general fund resources

## Fee Revenue

Comprehensive fee structure generates significant non-tax revenue:
- Building permits (value-based)
- Planning/zoning applications ($35-$10,000)
- Emergency medical services ($550-$750 base rates)
- Recreation facilities and programs

**Impact:** Fee revenue reduces pressure on property/income taxes to fund education.

## Municipal Tax Variations

Property tax rates vary by municipality:
- **Easton:** $0.6596 per $100 (lowest)
- **Oxford:** $0.6746 per $100
- **St. Michaels:** $0.6656 per $100
- **Queen Anne:** $0.7325 per $100
- **Trappe:** $0.6946 per $100

Lower rates in municipalities reflect dual taxation (county + town services).

## Analysis & Context

### Strengths
1. **High education priority:** 38.1% of budget
2. **Stable tax environment:** Minimal rate changes
3. **Low debt service:** 1.6% provides borrowing capacity
4. **Enterprise self-sufficiency:** Airport and utilities don't drain general fund

### Concerns
1. **Reserve dependence:** $21.7M one-time money balancing budget
2. **Long-term sustainability:** What happens when reserves depleted?
3. **Capital investment modest:** Only $260K for education capital
4. **No year-over-year comparison:** Cannot assess spending trends

### Infrastructure Investment Pattern
Analyst notes: "Significant capital spending on facilities and roads indicates deferred maintenance catch-up"

**But:** Education receives minimal capital allocation, suggesting either:
- School facilities in good condition
- Capital needs being addressed through separate bonding
- Deferring school infrastructure needs

## Key Questions

1. **Sustainability:** How many years can $21.7M reserve draw continue?
2. **Capital needs:** Are school facilities adequately maintained?
3. **Spending trends:** Is education budget growing or stable? (no prior year data)
4. **Blueprint compliance:** Meeting Maryland's increased education funding mandates?

## Bottom Line

Talbot County demonstrates **strong education funding commitment** (38.1% of budget, second-highest among peers), but faces **sustainability questions** due to:

- Heavy reliance on reserves ($21.7M) to balance budget
- Minimal capital investment in education facilities ($260K)
- No clear plan for when reserves are exhausted
- Stable tax rates suggest unwillingness to raise revenue

**Contrast with peers:**
- Similar education priority to Queen Anne's (39.9%)
- But Queen Anne's achieves balance without reserves
- Talbot's approach may be unsustainable long-term

**Analyst's summary:** "This budget reflects a county maintaining services while managing growth pressures, with particular emphasis on education and infrastructure investment while keeping tax rates relatively stable."

**The sustainability question remains unanswered.**
