# Eastern Shore Education Beatbook  

## How to use this beatbook  
This document is a “one‑stop‑shop” for a reporter new to the Eastern Shore K‑12 beat. Start by reading the **regional overview** to grasp the big‑picture forces that shape every county—Blueprint mandates, fiscal strain, staffing crunches, and emerging equity concerns. Then dive into each county section in the order the stories appear on the board calendar (Caroline, Dorchester, Kent, Queen Anne’s, Talbot). Use the **Big picture** paragraph to get a sense of the political climate and the key players you’ll meet at board meetings, town halls, and community forums. The **Top current issues & storylines** section tells you which of the nine major topics dominate recent coverage and where the reporting gaps are. Treat the **Power map** as a cheat sheet for who to call for a comment, a background interview, or a “walk‑through” of a school. Finally, the **Questions to walk in with** give you a starter list of angles to pursue in meetings or on‑the‑ground reporting. Keep the **Sources** list handy for quick links to budgets, data dashboards, and recurring interviewees.

## Regional overview: Five‑county picture  

The five Eastern Shore counties—Caroline, Dorchester, Kent, Queen Anne’s, and Talbot—spend a larger share of their local budgets on education than many western Maryland jurisdictions, but the exact percentages vary and are not publicly broken out in the data we have. All five districts are under the same **Blueprint for Maryland’s Future** (the Blueprint) framework, which mandates a 10‑year, $3.9 billion state investment and an 8 % local funding lift.  

**Shared pressures**  
* **Blueprint implementation** – Every county must roll out new early‑learning, literacy, math, and career‑technical pathways while meeting teacher‑salary floors (a $60 k minimum start‑salary by 2026) and career‑ladder bonuses. The state’s Accountability and Implementation Board (AIB) is reviewing each district’s plan, creating a common deadline pressure.  
* **Fiscal stress** – State and federal aid shortfalls (e.g., the $2 million federal cut in Dorchester; the $300 million statewide gap in April 2025) have forced boards to re‑allocate reserves, trim extracurriculars, and consider levy requests. Caroline’s superintendent called 2025 a “good budget year” only because of a one‑time state aid boost; Dorchester is already projecting a $12 million shortfall for FY 2026.  
* **Staffing shortages** – Blueprint teacher‑pay raises and the demand for National Board‑certified educators are outpacing the supply of qualified candidates. Dorchester is actively cutting staff to balance its budget, while Talbot is already lobbying for a $12 million increase just to keep pace with enrollment growth.  
* **Facility needs** – Aging school buildings and enrollment spikes are prompting multi‑million construction plans (Lockerman Middle in Caroline; a $45 million facilities master plan in Dorchester; Chapel Elementary renovations in Talbot). Safety upgrades (HVAC, security cameras, window replacements) are frequently bundled with capital projects.

Overall, the beat feels like a **race against time and money**: districts scramble to meet Blueprint deadlines while battling dwindling state aid and rising construction costs. The story angles that keep resurfacing are the same—budget‑vs‑staffing trade‑offs, equity‑focused discipline policies, and the tug‑of‑war between safety‑driven facility upgrades and the need for instructional space.

## Caroline County  

### Big picture: What this beat feels like on the ground  
Caroline County Public Schools (CCPS) serve a mix of small‑town communities and a rapidly growing western fringe. The board, led by President Michele Wayman (elected Dec 2024) and a slate of new members from health‑care and nonprofit sectors, leans toward collaborative, data‑driven decision‑making. Superintendent Derek Simmons is a frequent media face, emphasizing “Blueprint compliance” and “community partnership” in every press release. The County Commissioners are also active, especially on the FY 26 budget for Chesapeake College. The relationship between the school system and the county government is generally cooperative, but tensions flare when state funding cuts threaten local construction or staffing plans.  

### Budget, Blueprint & fiscal stress  
* **Education share of the county budget** – Not disclosed in the source material; you’ll need the county’s FY 2025 Comprehensive Annual Financial Report to get the exact % of total spending that goes to K‑12.  
* **Blueprint impact** – CCPS has a 40.3 % per‑pupil funding increase (FY 19‑24) and expects a “good budget year” thanks to a $45 million capital allocation and $12 million for instruction (Dec 2024). The district is still awaiting technical assistance to flesh out a teacher career ladder and to expand its three‑year literacy plan.  
* **Fiscal stress points** – Early Head Start funding will be cut by $2 million in FY 26, risking two preschool sites and 180 children. The board approved a $1.2 million recreation‑education partnership (Sept 2025) and a $12 million bond‑funded Lockerman Middle School, but both rely on state aid and bond capacity.  

### Student outcomes, equity & discipline  
* **Achievement** – MCAP 2024‑25 reading scores fell 2 points; math was flat (Aug 2025). The district’s own data show a modest 17 % drop in phone‑related incidents after re‑introducing cellphone pouches, and early signs of a math score bump in the middle schools that used the pouches.  
* **Equity gaps** – The Early Head Start cut disproportionately affects low‑income families, and the Board’s recent discipline review (Oct 2025) is intended to surface racial and socioeconomic disparities across all five Eastern Shore counties.  
* **Discipline** – The phone‑pouch policy (Aug 2025) was a direct response to a spike in distractions and cyber‑bullying; the board is now commissioning a district‑wide discipline audit.  

### Top current issues & storylines (organized by major topics)  

**School Board/Policy** – The board’s agenda is dominated by infrastructure (Lockerman Middle), recreation‑education partnerships, and the upcoming discipline data review (Oct 2025). Board turnover (three new members Dec 2024) has introduced fresh community‑health perspectives.  

**Curriculum/Programs** – The district is pushing pre‑K expansion, STEM labs (Lockerman’s $2.1 M STEM Center), and a renewed emphasis on early‑literacy. The “Girls Lose Gains in Math and Science” story (Sept 2025) highlights a regional gender‑equity issue that Caroline has yet to address directly.  

**Staffing** – Teacher recruitment remains a concern. The Blueprint calls for a career ladder, but CCPS still lacks a finalized plan. The phone‑pouch rollout required additional staff monitoring, revealing understaffed support roles.  

**Budget/Funding** – The $12 M Lockerman Middle allocation, $1.2 M recreation‑education partnership, and the looming $2 M Early Head Start cut illustrate a balancing act between capital projects and operating needs.  

**Achievement/Scores** – Mixed results: a dip in reading, stable math, but a modest 17 % reduction in phone‑related disruptions that correlates with slight math gains in middle schools.  

**Equity** – The discipline data review (Oct 2025) will compare suspension rates across race and poverty. Early‑Head Start cuts could widen the preschool readiness gap.  

**Construction/Facilities** – Lockerman Middle (new site selected, design underway) and the $12 M Capital Improvement Program dominate the construction narrative. Safety upgrades (e.g., HVAC, security) are bundled with these projects.  

**Behavior/Discipline** – The re‑introduction of cellphone pouches and the upcoming county‑wide discipline audit suggest a shift toward data‑driven behavior policy.  

**Safety** – Facility upgrades are often justified on safety grounds (e.g., newer HVAC for indoor‑air quality). The PowerSchool data breach (Jan 2025) forced the district to overhaul cybersecurity protocols, a story that still has follow‑up potential.  

**Dominant topics** – Board policy (27 stories), Curriculum/Programs (26), and Staffing (22) dominate coverage, indicating a strong focus on how Blueprint mandates translate into classroom reality. Under‑reported but important: **Equity** (9 stories) and **Behavior/Discipline** (7 stories).  

### Power map: Who matters and how to cover them  
* **Superintendent Derek Simmons** – Primary spokesperson for Blueprint compliance, construction, and policy. He holds weekly briefings and is often quoted on budget matters.  
* **Board President Michele Wayman** – Leads board meetings; her background in community health informs the recreation‑education partnership.  
* **Director of Operations Bill Mengel** – Oversees construction projects; key for facility‑related questions.  
* **Director of Student Services Darlene Spurrier** (Talbot) – Though not Caroline, her role in the regional discipline review makes her a useful cross‑county contact.  
* **County Commissioners (Travis Breeding, Larry Porter)** – Control FY 26 budget for Chesapeake College; useful for higher‑ed linkage stories.  
* **Teachers’ Union (Caroline County Education Association)** – Will be vocal on career‑ladder implementation and teacher‑salary hikes.  
* **Community groups** – Caroline County Recreation and Parks (after‑school partnership), local health clinics (for student wellness data).  

Board meetings are the first Monday of each month; the **Operations Committee** meets the third Wednesday, and a **Construction Advisory Committee** convenes quarterly.

### Questions to walk in with  
1. **Budget‑vs‑Blueprint trade‑offs** – How does the district plan to fund the teacher career ladder and expanded pre‑K while the state trims Blueprint dollars? What contingency plans exist if the $2 M Early Head Start cut becomes permanent?  
2. **Equity in discipline** – What preliminary findings have emerged from the multi‑county discipline data review? Are there disproportional suspension rates for students of color or low‑income status, and how will policies like the phone‑pouch system address those gaps?  
3. **Construction timing and safety** – With Lockerman Middle slated for 2028 opening, what interim solutions are in place for the current overcrowded middle schools? How are safety upgrades (e.g., HVAC, security cameras) being prioritized in the capital budget?  
4. **Staffing pipeline** – What specific technical assistance is the district receiving from the AIB to develop its career ladder? Are there partnerships with local colleges (e.g., Chesapeake College) to create a pipeline for future teachers?

## Dorchester County  

### Big picture: What this beat feels like on the ground  
Dorchester County Public Schools (DCPS) cover a largely rural, high‑poverty landscape. Superintendent **Jymil Thompson** is a frequent media presence, championing data‑driven “student‑accountability” systems and community‑school models. The Board of Education is a tight‑knit group (President Sheri Hubbard) with a strong focus on **equity**—the district’s 2024‑25 MCAP gains were framed as a victory for targeted tutoring and broadband expansion. County Council members (e.g., Lenny Pfeffer) sit on education budget committees, creating a layered governance structure where school‑board decisions often need council sign‑off.  

### Budget, Blueprint & fiscal stress  
* **Education share of county budget** – Not provided; request the Dorchester County Comprehensive Annual Financial Report for the exact percentage.  
* **Blueprint impact** – Per‑pupil funding rose 36.9 % (FY 19‑24). The district received a $45 M Facilities Master Plan (July 2025) and a $3.2 M technology upgrade (Sept 2025). However, a **$4.3 M deficit** for FY 24‑25 forced the Board to cancel summer school (Mar 2025) and consider staff reductions (Mar 2025).  
* **Fiscal stress points** – Federal aid cuts of $2 M (July 2025) forced a $800 k re‑allocation to health services. A revised Blueprint cost estimate (March 2025) exceeds 2022 projections, prompting council‑board talks about a supplemental levy.  

### Student outcomes, equity & discipline  
* **Achievement** – Dramatic gains: reading proficiency up 15 % to 68 %; math up 10 % to 61 % (Aug 2025). Graduation rate hit 92 % (2025). However, chronic absenteeism, while down to 4.2 %, remains above the state target.  
* **Equity** – Gains were most pronounced in schools with new broadband and tutoring programs, suggesting technology access is a key equity lever.  
* **Discipline** – A new cell‑phone policy (Aug 2025) aims to curb cyber‑bullying; early data show modest compliance but no published impact on suspension rates yet.  

### Top current issues & storylines  

**School Board/Policy** – The Board’s policy committee (Sept 2025) adopted attendance tech, a $3.2 M tech upgrade, and STEM PD mandates. The July 2025 budget re‑allocation after federal cuts shows policy decisions are increasingly driven by funding volatility.  

**Curriculum/Programs** – The “23, No More, Believe in Us” initiative (augmented STEM labs, tutoring) is credited for the test‑score surge. However, the district still lacks a fully‑articulated math plan, a Blueprint requirement flagged in the analysis.  

**Staffing** – Staff reductions are on the table (Mar 2025) to offset a projected $3.2 M shortfall, targeting teachers and support staff. Retention of National Board‑certified teachers remains a challenge; the district’s recruitment pipeline is thin.  

**Budget/Funding** – The $45 M Facilities Master Plan (July 2025) and $3.2 M tech upgrade are major capital outlays. Yet the district is grappling with a $4.3 M operating deficit, prompting cuts to after‑school programs and a cancellation of summer school.  

**Achievement/Scores** – MCAP data (Aug 2025) show the largest gains in a decade, especially in reading (+15 %). The district attributes success to targeted tutoring and broadband expansion.  

**Equity** – The technology upgrade and the “student‑accountability” system are framed as equity tools. Yet the early‑Head Start cut in neighboring counties and the district’s own staffing gaps risk widening gaps for low‑income students.  

**Construction/Facilities** – The Facilities Master Plan (45 M) includes a new middle‑school campus, elementary renovations, and HVAC upgrades to meet health standards. Construction timelines stretch to 2029, creating a long‑term capital horizon.  

**Behavior/Discipline** – The cell‑phone policy (Aug 2025) and a revised attendance policy (Sept 2025) are the district’s primary discipline levers. No recent suspension data are available, a gap that a reporter could fill.  

**Safety** – Facility upgrades (HVAC, ventilation) are justified on post‑pandemic health safety. The PowerSchool breach (Jan 2025) also raised data‑security concerns.  

**Dominant topics** – School Board/Policy (30 stories) and Curriculum/Programs (29) dominate coverage, reflecting a focus on governance and instructional reform. Under‑reported but critical: **Construction/Facilities** (7) and **Behavior/Discipline** (8) – especially the impact of policy changes on student outcomes.  

### Power map: Who matters and how to cover them  
* **Superintendent Jymil Thompson** – Central spokesperson on Blueprint, equity, and budget crises.  
* **Board President Sheri Hubbard** – Leads policy decisions, especially around technology and discipline.  
* **Board Finance Chair Chris Wheedleton** – Primary contact for budget reallocations and levy discussions.  
* **County Council President Lenny Pfeffer** – Influences supplemental levy and state‑funding negotiations.  
* **Dorchester Educators Union (DEU)** – Will push back on staff reductions; good source for teacher‑voice stories.  
* **Community partners** – Robbins YMCA Reading Hub (May 2025) and local businesses sponsoring STEM labs.  

Board meetings occur the first Tuesday of each month; a **Finance Committee** meets the third Thursday, and the **Facilities Master Plan** is reviewed quarterly by an inter‑agency construction committee.  

### Sources, documents & links  
- Dorchester County Board of Education website – agendas, minutes, budget PDFs.  
- MSDE Blueprint dashboards (implementation status, per‑pupil funding).  
- MCAP 2024‑25 performance report (state release, Aug 2025).  
- Dorchester County Comprehensive Annual Financial Report (FY 2025).  
- PowerSchool breach notice and remediation plan (Jan 2025).  
- “23, No More, Believe in Us” initiative documentation (Fall 2024).  
- Facilities Master Plan (July 2025) – PDF.  
- Robbins YMCA Reading Hub grant announcement (May 2025).  

### Questions to walk in with  
1. **Funding gap accounting** – How does the district reconcile the $45 M Facilities Master Plan with a $4.3 M operating deficit? What is the timeline for securing the additional $12 M Blueprint shortfall identified in March 2025?  
2. **Staffing cuts impact** – Which student services (e.g., tutoring, special‑education) are most at risk if the proposed 45‑position reduction proceeds? Can the district quantify projected effects on MCAP scores?  
3. **Equity of technology** – The $3.2 M technology upgrade targeted 45 schools; how are devices and broadband distributed to ensure low‑income students benefit equally?  
4. **Discipline data transparency** – The Board has not released recent suspension or exclusion statistics. Will the upcoming discipline audit (Oct 2025) publish disaggregated data by race, disability, and poverty?

## Kent County  

### Big picture: What this beat feels like on the ground  
Kent County Public Schools (KCPS) are a **small‑district** with a tight budget and a strong community‑school ethos. Superintendent **Dr. Karen Couch** announced her retirement in early 2024, leaving the board (President Joe Goetz) to conduct a statewide search. The district’s leadership emphasizes **universal pre‑K** and **mentor‑teacher programs**, but faces chronic staffing shortages in math, world languages, and secondary educators. Community partners (e.g., local colleges) are leveraged for AP staffing, but the rural setting limits private‑sector involvement.  

### Budget, Blueprint & fiscal stress  
* **Education share of county budget** – Not publicly disclosed; request the Kent County FY 2025 Comprehensive Annual Financial Report.  
* **Blueprint impact** – Per‑pupil funding rose 33.8 % (FY 19‑24). The district has a **teacher‑career‑ladder** mentor program but has not yet expanded beyond National Board certification incentives. No major capital projects are listed in the recent news set, suggesting limited construction activity.  
* **Fiscal stress** – The board’s FY 2025 budget (May 2024) shows modest increases, but the district is still wrestling with **teacher‑salary floor compliance** (the 10 % raise due June 2024). No large‑scale deficits have been reported, but the lack of capital funding could become a future bottleneck.  

### Student outcomes, equity & discipline  
* **Achievement** – Only two MCAP stories (2024‑25) were identified; the district posted stable proficiency rates, trailing neighboring counties. The **math plan** remains “in development,” a Blueprint requirement that is a known weakness.  
* **Equity** – Universal pre‑K for 4‑year‑olds (and expanding to 3‑year‑olds) is a strength, but the lack of robust CTE pathways and limited business partnerships may disadvantage students seeking post‑secondary technical careers.  
* **Discipline** – Only one story (behavior/discipline) in the last year, indicating either low incident rates or under‑reporting.  

### Top current issues & storylines  

**School Board/Policy** – The board’s 2024‑25 budget and the upcoming superintendent search dominate headlines. Board members are debating how to fund the teacher‑salary increase and whether to expand the career‑ladder beyond National Board certification.  

**Curriculum/Programs** – The district is **finalizing a math plan** (Blueprint requirement) and expanding universal pre‑K. The “Community Schools” partnership with neighboring Cecil County is a notable equity‑focused program.  

**Staffing** – Shortages in math, world language, and secondary teachers are acute. The district relies on mentor programs and college partnerships, but the pipeline is thin.  

**Budget/Funding** – No large capital projects are reported; the main fiscal conversation revolves around meeting the 10 % teacher‑salary increase and funding pre‑K expansion.  

**Achievement/Scores** – MCAP results are relatively flat; the district’s “closing gaps” narrative (July 2025) is modest compared to Dorchester’s dramatic gains.  

**Equity** – Universal pre‑K is a high‑impact equity lever, but the lack of CTE and the need for a robust math plan leave a gap for low‑income students.  

**Construction/Facilities** – Five stories, but none describe new construction; likely routine maintenance or small upgrades.  

**Behavior/Discipline** – Only one story; a potential reporting gap.  

**Safety** – One story (likely related to a specific incident) – another under‑reported area.  

**Dominant topics** – The coverage is balanced across the nine topics (5 stories each for Board, Curriculum, Staffing, Budget, Construction, and 2–3 for Achievement, Equity, Safety). The low story counts for **Achievement** and **Equity** suggest an opportunity to dig into how the universal pre‑K is affecting long‑term outcomes and how the missing math plan is impacting student readiness.  

### Power map: Who matters and how to cover them  
* **Superintendent Dr. Karen Couch** – Outgoing superintendent; her exit will shape the district’s future direction.  
* **Board President Joe Goetz** – Leads the search; key for budget approval and policy decisions.  
* **Vice President Francoise Sullivan** – Often speaks on finance and capital projects.  
* **Kent County Education Association (KCEA)** – Represents teachers; will be vocal on salary‑floor compliance and staffing.  
* **Local community colleges (e.g., Salisbury University)** – Partners for AP and CTE courses.  

Board meetings are the first Thursday of each month; the **Finance Committee** meets the third Tuesday.  

### Sources, documents & links  
- Kent County Board of Education website – meeting minutes, FY 2025 budget.  
- MSDE Blueprint implementation portal – Kent County plan status.  
- Kent County Comprehensive Annual Financial Report (FY 2025).  
- MCAP 2024‑25 results for Kent County (state release).  
- Community Schools partnership agreement with Cecil County (2024).  

### Questions to walk in with  
1. **Math plan timeline** – When will the district finalize and implement its Blueprint‑required math plan, and what resources are allocated for it?  
2. **Teacher‑salary floor** – How will the district meet the 10 % salary increase deadline if state aid does not cover the shortfall? Are there contingency plans (e.g., phased raises, targeted incentives)?  
3. **CTE pipeline** – Given the lack of local businesses, what innovative partnerships (online, regional, or state‑wide) are being explored to expand career‑technical education?  
4. **Discipline data** – Why are there so few recent discipline stories? Can the district provide recent suspension/exclusion data disaggregated by race and poverty?

## Queen Anne’s County  

### Big picture: What this beat feels like on the ground  
Queen Anne’s County Public Schools (QACPS) serve a relatively affluent suburban‑rural mix, with a **high per‑pupil funding increase of only 25.4 %**, the lowest on the Eastern Shore. The Board of Education (President Shannon Bent) has a reputation for being data‑driven but also **cautious on spending**—the recent denial of a classical charter school (Sept 2025) illustrates a “risk‑averse” board culture. Interim Superintendent **Matthew Kibler** is navigating a transition after Dr. Karen Couch’s retirement in Kent and is focused on meeting the 10 % teacher‑salary increase deadline, which the district currently cannot meet.  

### Budget, Blueprint & fiscal stress  
* **Education share of county budget** – Not disclosed; obtain the county’s FY 2025 financial statements.  
* **Blueprint impact** – Per‑pupil funding up 25.4 % (FY 19‑24). The district is **behind** on the teacher‑salary floor (June 2024) and is negotiating a new arbitration agreement. Funding gaps are evident: a $13 M budget approved in August 2024 left **$45 M unfunded** for mandated Blueprint programs.  
* **Fiscal stress points** – The board’s decision to reject the Classical Charter School (Sept 2025) was partially driven by concerns about meeting **College and Career Ready standards** without additional funding. The county audit (Sept 2024) of school‑system finances indicates potential overspending or misallocation, fueling community calls for greater transparency.  

### Student outcomes, equity & discipline  
* **Achievement** – MCAP data (2024‑25) show **78 % proficiency**, the highest among the Eastern Shore counties, but the district still lags in **math staffing** and **secondary teacher recruitment**.  
* **Equity** – The district’s **universal pre‑K** for 4‑year‑olds (and pilots for 3‑year‑olds) is a strength, yet gaps remain in **math** and **world‑language** courses, especially for low‑income students.  
* **Discipline** – Only one recent discipline story (the charter school denial included an equity review). No major discipline policy changes have been reported, suggesting either stability or a lack of reporting.  

### Top current issues & storylines  

**School Board/Policy** – The board’s most visible move has been the **charter school denial** (Sept 2025) and the **financial audit** (Sept 2024). Both illustrate a cautious approach to expansion amid budget gaps.  

**Curriculum/Programs** – The district boasts a **comprehensive math plan** and an updated literacy plan, yet staffing shortages in math and world languages undermine implementation. The **dual‑enrollment improvement process** is a notable success story.  

**Staffing** – The district is **unable** to meet the 10 % teacher‑salary increase by June 2024. Recruitment for math, world‑language, and secondary teachers is a chronic issue.  

**Budget/Funding** – The $13 M budget (Aug 2024) left $45 M unfunded for Blueprint mandates—a stark illustration of the funding gap. The audit (Sept 2024) may uncover overspending, which could affect future levy requests.  

**Achievement/Scores** – MCAP proficiency at 78 % is the highest in the region, but the district’s **math achievement** lags behind the state average, and the **teacher‑salary floor** issue threatens future gains.  

**Equity** – Universal pre‑K is a strong equity lever, but the inability to staff math and world‑language classes may disproportionately affect low‑income and minority students.  

**Construction/Facilities** – Three construction stories (including the charter school site review) indicate a modest capital pipeline, mostly focused on meeting safety and capacity standards.  

**Behavior/Discipline** – Minimal coverage; a potential blind spot.  

**Safety** – The charter denial cited **safety‑standard compliance** concerns. No major safety upgrades reported recently.  

**Dominant topics** – Board/Policy (4 stories) and Staffing (5) dominate coverage; **Equity** (3) and **Safety** (3) are under‑reported given the district’s socioeconomic mix.  

### Power map: Who matters and how to cover them  
* **Interim Superintendent Matthew Kibler** – Handles day‑to‑day operations and negotiations with the teachers’ union.  
* **Board President Shannon Bent** – Leads the board’s cautious fiscal stance; often the spokesperson on charter decisions.  
* **Chief Financial Officer (CFO) – likely a county administrator** – Key for audit findings and budget reconciliation.  
* **Queen Anne’s County Education Association (QACEA) President Cecilia Mitchell** – Will be vocal on teacher‑salary floor and staffing shortages.  
* **County Commissioners (Jim Moran, Chris Corchiarino, Jack Wilson)** – Approved the independent audit; can be asked about oversight and future levy considerations.  

Board meetings: first Tuesday of each month; Finance Committee meets third Thursday.

## Talbot County  

### Big picture: What this beat feels like on the ground  
Talbot County Public Schools (TCPS) operate in a **wealthy‑by‑regional‑standards** county but face sharp **equity challenges** (over 50 % of students qualify for free/reduced lunch, rising homelessness). Superintendent **Sharon Pepukayi** is a high‑profile figure, frequently quoted on Blueprint implementation, AI policy, and immigration‑related safety concerns. The Board of Education (President Emily Jackson) has a **progressive tilt**—new AI guidelines, community‑school expansion, and a strong focus on gender‑equity (girls’ STEM decline). Recent elections brought in several new board members (Nov 2024), shifting the board’s composition toward a more **Blueprint‑driven agenda**.  

### Budget, Blueprint & fiscal stress  
* **Education share of county budget** – Not explicitly provided; request the Talbot County FY 2025 Comprehensive Annual Financial Report.  
* **Blueprint impact** – Per‑pupil funding increased **49 %** (FY 19‑24), the highest among the five counties. The district’s FY 2026 budget (Feb 2025) totals **$215 M**, with **$45 M** earmarked for Blueprint initiatives (technology upgrades, teacher salary bumps, early‑learning expansion).
