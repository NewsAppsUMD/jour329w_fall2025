# A Guide to Sports Journalism: Covering the Mid-Atlantic Region

Welcome to sports journalism in the Maryland-DC-Virginia area. This region offers a rich landscape of stories spanning professional teams, college athletics, high school sports, and emerging trends that are reshaping athletics at every level. Let me walk you through what matters and where the opportunities lie.

## Understanding the Territory

You're covering a region with deep sports traditions and significant ongoing change. The professional teams—the Capitals, Wizards, Nationals, Commanders, Orioles, and Ravens—provide the obvious headlines, but some of the most compelling stories exist in the spaces between the major leagues. Pay attention to the infrastructure: venues like FedEx Field face criticism over home-field advantage due to visiting fans, while new facilities like Audi Field (opened 2018 for D.C. United) and proposed renovations at Pimlico Race Course signal shifts in how teams and cities think about the fan experience.

The college scene centers on Maryland athletics, which competes in the Big Ten, creating natural tensions and storylines around conference realignment. Johns Hopkins maintains its lacrosse dynasty. Lesser-covered programs at schools like Howard, Morgan State, Towson, and Coppin State offer opportunities to explore HBCU athletics and mid-major basketball.

## Name, Image, and Likeness: The Story That Keeps Evolving

One of the most significant ongoing stories is how NIL (Name, Image, and Likeness) deals are transforming high school and college sports. Maryland changed its rules in December 2022 to permit high school athletes to profit from NIL, with volleyball star Alexis Ewing from Bullis School becoming one of the first to sign a deal (with College HUNKS Hauling Junk & Moving). 

The Shirley Povich Center conducted a four-month investigation revealing a patchwork of state policies. As of their reporting, 41 states and D.C. allowed high school NIL deals, but restrictions vary wildly—from reporting requirements to prohibitions on mentioning schools or teams, and bans on endorsing products like tobacco or alcohol.

**What you should watch for:**
- How NIL might be influencing high school transfers, particularly at competitive programs like DeMatha Catholic
- The need for education programs for athletes and parents (North Carolina has mandatory programs; most states don't)
- Whether Little League World Series will clarify its murky NIL rules for youth athletes
- The expansion of businesses like Portland Gear partnering with high school athletes

**Story angles to pursue:**
- Profile Maryland athletes who've secured deals and track their experiences
- Investigate whether NIL is actually affecting recruitment and retention at local high schools
- Follow up on which state athletic associations have implemented education programs
- Examine the socioeconomic dimensions—are NIL opportunities distributed equitably?

## Youth Sports: Safety, Access, and Opportunity

Youth tackle football presents a complex story about risk, race, and community. The Povich Center investigation found that Black and Hispanic parents are nearly twice as likely as white parents to view youth football as a path to college scholarships and the NFL, despite widespread awareness of injury risks. In communities like Lexington, Mississippi, football offers limited opportunities for advancement, while wealthier Atlanta suburbs see it as tradition worth the risk.

Polling data shows 84% of Americans support playing the national anthem before sporting events, though 23% of Black Americans find it hurtful or unrepresentative—a nuance worth exploring when covering any athlete protests or demonstrations.

**Undercovered angles:**
- How Maryland's diverse communities approach youth sports differently
- The growth of alternative sports like pickleball (mentioned as growing rapidly in Montgomery County)
- Cricket's expansion through the Maryland Youth Cricket Association (grew from 50 to over 1,000 participants in a year)
- The Maryland Black Bears junior hockey team offering pathways to college for local players

## Professional Teams: Beyond the Box Scores

### Baseball's Numbers Game

Both Baltimore and Washington baseball teams offer statistical stories worth pursuing. The Nationals won their first World Series in 2019 but experienced a historic five-year decline (.407 winning percentage, second-worst post-championship slump since 1900). The Orioles have produced over 300 NFL draft picks from Maryland colleges since 1936, yet lag behind other teams in international scouting—they were the only MLB team not to sign international players in summer 2017.

The 2019 season saw a massive home run surge (6,776 homers, shattering records), with theories ranging from altered baseballs to strategic shifts. This isn't just trivia—it's about how the game is changing fundamentally.

**Story ideas:**
- Track the Orioles' international scouting strategy as it evolves
- Examine how new MLB rules (pitch clock, larger bases, shift restrictions) are affecting play at all levels
- Profile Maryland prospects in minor league systems

### Basketball's Evolving Landscape

The Wizards and Capitals dominate winter coverage, but pay attention to attendance patterns. Maryland basketball games at Xfinity Center often look empty despite having one of the Big Ten's largest arenas. The Wizards face questions about home-court advantage, with John Wall's injury history and defensive focus providing ongoing angles.

The WNBA's Mystics don't receive proportional coverage despite competitive success. Women's basketball generally presents opportunities for differentiated reporting—Maryland women's basketball saw attendance increases but still lags far behind men's programs.

### Football's Quarterback Economics

The NFL's quarterback salary structures deserve scrutiny. Teams are "breaking the bank on average quarterbacks," with players like Matthew Stafford and Derek Carr receiving elite-level contracts despite inconsistent performance compared to Tom Brady and Aaron Rodgers. The Ravens' Joe Flacco had career-worst deep passing struggles (as of 2017 reporting), while Carson Wentz arrived in Washington with fanfare that proved premature.

## Lacrosse: Maryland's Sport

Lacrosse is Maryland's signature sport, with the University of Maryland-Johns Hopkins rivalry dating back decades as "the oldest in the sport." Maryland won a national championship, Georgetown hired Caitlyn Phipps (who led Maryland to five NCAA championships over a decade), and local high schools consistently produce Division I talent.

The Premier Lacrosse League announced plans for competition during COVID, demonstrating how the sport adapted. Bill Belichick maintains his lacrosse roots through frequent Johns Hopkins visits, connecting NFL success to Maryland's lacrosse culture.

**Story opportunities:**
- Profile the pipeline from Maryland high schools to Division I programs
- Cover wheelchair lacrosse and adaptive sports (Caleb Krewson scored his first high school goal)
- Track how women's lacrosse coaching trees spread from Maryland programs

## Issues and Institutions to Understand

### The Coaching Network

Jay Gruden built his Washington NFL staff largely through pre-existing relationships from working with his brother Jon Gruden, illustrating how NFL coaching networks function. This "coaching tree" concept applies across sports and creates story opportunities about mentorship, hiring patterns, and institutional knowledge transfer.

### Diversity in Leadership

The Big Ten has worked to increase diversity among head coaches, with figures like Mike Locksley (Maryland football), Marisa Moseley (formerly at Wisconsin), and Commissioner Kevin Warren representing progress. However, the coaching carousel in soccer shows high turnover rates, with MLS and top European leagues cycling through managers rapidly.

### Climate Change and Sports

Expect climate change to affect sports infrastructure, with predictions including more domed stadiums for baseball and fewer viable ski resorts. Extreme weather already impacts scheduling and fan attendance.

### Gambling's Spread

Sports betting legalization continues evolving. Maryland launched retail sports betting in December 2021, but mobile options faced regulatory delays. Universities like Colorado Boulder partnered with betting companies (PointsBet), raising questions about marketing to students under legal gambling age. Most Americans approve of legalized betting on professional sports, but concerns remain about college sports due to athlete exploitation fears.

## What to Avoid

**Don't assume stories are finished.** The Pimlico renovation was approved for a three-year timeline, but construction projects face delays. The Commanders' quarterback situation remains perpetually unsettled. Maryland football investigations concluded with coach DJ Durkin being allowed to return before public pressure forced his departure—these situations evolve rapidly.

**Don't ignore attendance realities.** NCAA rules allow colleges to report distributed tickets rather than actual attendance, creating inflated figures. Maryland reported a 56.3% difference between announced and scanned attendance in 2018. Always ask for turnstile counts.

**Don't overlook the business side.** The economic impact of events matters—the Nationals' 2019 World Series run generated an estimated $6.5 million for D.C.'s economy in one game. New stadiums, ticket promotions, and corporate partnerships drive organizational decisions.

**Don't forget historical context.** The Washington franchise's name change, Jordan McNair's tragic death from heatstroke at Maryland football practice, and ongoing questions about athlete safety in college football (22 Division I players died from overexertion since 2000) inform current coverage.

## Practical Reporting Tips

**For game coverage**: Stats tell stories, but context matters more. John Wall's MVP-caliber 2017 season was overshadowed by Russell Westbrook and James Harden's historic years. Anthony Rendon lacked MVP buzz despite elite performance because narratives hadn't caught up to his numbers. Look beyond basic box scores.

**For investigative work**: The Povich Center's NIL investigation and youth football project demonstrate the value of sustained, multi-month reporting with polling data to support observations. Partner with data journalists when possible.

**For features**: The best stories connect sports to larger themes—Mo Creek trapped in Ukraine during the Russian invasion, Nancy Heil's racehorse saving her farm, Emma Hayes's path to USWNT coaching success. Look for universal themes through sports specifics.

**For breaking news**: Social media provides real-time reaction, but verify before publishing. The trade of Joe Flacco to Denver, Bryce Harper's free agency, and coaching changes generate immediate hot takes that need context and confirmation.
